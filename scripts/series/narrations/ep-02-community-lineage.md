Episode Two: What Chicago Code Camp was really about — the people, the sponsors, and the three-year May conference rhythm captured in 796 commits.

<!-- cue:three-mays -->
Look at the commit cadence in git and you can feel the heartbeat of a free, community-run Saturday conference. In 2010: 175 commits from January to May, launching on Rails 2.3.5 with GPL v2 locked in on April 7. In 2011: 489 commits — 155 in February alone — rebuilding everything on Rails 3. And in 2012: 132 commits right up to May 15, four days before doors opened at the College of Lake County in Grayslake, Illinois.

<!-- cue:contributors -->
Four contributors appear in git history up to prod-2012-05: Michael D. Hall with 764 commits, Sergio Pereira with 24 — including two early 2010 commits from Windows where git fell back to unknown — Adam Walters with 7, and Dustin Updyke with 1. And right in the footer, Jane Ashley is credited for the three-ring dot logo.

<!-- cue:sponsors-2012 -->
Free conferences don't run on air. The May 6, 2012 Internet Archive capture preserves the twelve sponsors who backed Chicago Code Camp 2012: Microsoft at Platinum, Platinum Consulting Services at Gold, 8th Light, Centare, Groupon, LogicNP Software, Telerik, ThoughtWorks, and Twilio at Silver, plus GitHub, JetBrains, and Neudesic at Bronze.

<!-- cue:speakers-2012 -->
And the speakers who gave up a Saturday to teach: 32 speakers on the May 2012 lineup covering .NET, Ruby, Clojure, Go, iOS, Java, Node.js, and software craftsmanship. Early 2010 speaker headshots in git also preserve the memory of regional community builders including Dave Bost, whose warmth and contributions we honor respectfully.
